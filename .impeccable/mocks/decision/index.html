Five worlds, one page

Each frame below is the same first viewport — the LC 206 stepper at step 12, mid-unwind — rendered in a different visual world. Real HTML and CSS, not images, so what you see is what the build would be. Equal fidelity in each card’s own grammar.

The roll

The Lecture Board

Slate ground, pressed chalk. The surface where recursion is actually taught — built as software, not imitated as texture.

Watch for: phase colours the whole ground, one chalked claim per page, and no grey that the program did not put there.
Impeccable’s pick

The Schematic

Transit-diagram wayfinding. Fixed geometry, named lines, one legend that never lies.

Honest risk: this is where algorithm sites land once they get serious about colour-coding. Competent, not distinctive.
Competitive

The Select Rail

A film cutting bench flattened into a sheet. State is a mark, not a hue.

Its discipline: marks survive colour-blindness and the scrub snaps to step pitch. Its cost: one ink on black has no room for a third identity, which LC 21 needs.
Competitive

Thirty-Two Folds

One square of washi, numbered folds, nothing added or removed.

Its discipline: an uncut sheet is exactly what LC 206 does to a list, and every step is walk-back-able. Its cost: fits pointers, fits nothing in Week 9’s knapsack tables.
Competitive

The Fixed-Scale Folio

Every stage drawn at the same scale on the same ground, so change is the only thing that moves.

Its discipline: fixed scale makes two steps comparable at a glance. Its cost: warm cream and engraved labels is the look most models reach for unprompted.
